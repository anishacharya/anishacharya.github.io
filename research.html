A thematic reading of the work rather than the chronological one the CV gives. The threads are not independent, and the coupling is the point: search amplifies whatever bias the signal carries, so a stronger optimizer aimed at a corrupted objective is worse than a weaker one.

01

Recursive self improvement

Agent systems that propose changes to their own prompts, tools, and orchestration, score them, and keep what scored better. I work on both halves of that loop: the signal it optimizes against, and the search that exploits it. The arc runs from human-specified agent behaviour — Alexa Conversations, generated from hand-authored dialog templates — to self-modifying behaviour. That transition is why the earlier theory became load-bearing: once the human is out of the loop, nothing else is checking the objective.

02

Robust estimation under contamination

The methodological spine, stated as a problem rather than a method: a learning system is a chain of averaging steps, and the average is the least robust estimator there is. Recognise a step as mean estimation under contamination, then replace the naive estimator with one carrying a provable breakdown point. The same diagnosis has now been applied at three points in the pipeline, roughly one every three years — gradients across workers, sample means used for matching, and scores across a panel of judges — with the object different each time and the mathematics unchanged.

03

Optimization: non-convex, distributed, combinatorial

The other half of every result above: once you have decided what to estimate, something still has to find it. Three regimes recur. Distributed — what can be proved about convergence when workers are slow, sparsely connected, and possibly hostile; this is where the lesson that an aggregation rule is part of the estimator rather than part of the plumbing was learned, on gradients, years before it was applied to judges. Non-convex — most clean federated theory assumes convexity away, and the hard part is what remains when you don't. Combinatorial and constrained — prompt states, orchestration topology, and exactly-k subsets are discrete, and continuous relaxation is usually the wrong move because the constraints that matter are not ones you want to satisfy approximately.

04

Learning on a budget: bits, labels, samples, tokens

One question asked in four currencies over eleven years: you have a fixed budget, so where does the accuracy actually go? Model footprint and learned distributed compression spend bits; robust subset selection spends samples; learning from positive and unlabeled data spends no negative labels at all, because a non-click is not a negative and manufacturing negatives injects the very bias you were avoiding. The current form of the question is tokens — every iteration of a self-improving loop spends inference, evaluation, and context.

05

Origins: control, estimation, signal processing

The undergraduate work at Jadavpur was not machine learning. It was control theory: fractional-order analog filters, optimal PID control of chaotic Lorenz-like attractors, stability of delayed systems via Bode's integral, and system identification under fractional Gaussian noise. Four ideas were already present — identify the system before you optimize it; the noise model is a modelling choice; stability is not a nice-to-have; and optimal control is optimization against a dynamical objective. Swap "plant" for "learned model" and "controller" for "training procedure," and that is the current program almost verbatim.
