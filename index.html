About

I am a Senior Research Scientist at Amazon Web Services in Austin, Texas, where I work on recursive self-improvement loops for multi-agent swarms. I received my PhD in Electrical and Computer Engineering from The University of Texas at Austin in 2025, advised by Inderjit S. Dhillon and Sujay Sanghavi, with a thesis on Robust and Efficient Learning in High Dimensions from Noisy Data.

During my PhD I was a research scientist at Meta (Capacity Engineering and FAIR) and Microsoft (Speech and Dialog Research), and led AI research at Eidon AI. Before it, I spent three years at Amazon building NLP models for Alexa, moving from software engineering to applied science across Alexa ML, Alexa AI, and AGI.

Earlier roles include data science at eBay and Schlumberger and a visiting research position at the Toyota Technological Institute at Chicago with David McAllester. I hold an MS in EECS from UC Irvine and a BE from Jadavpur University.

Research

I am broadly interested in learning theory. My research studies learning as a feedback process under imperfect information. A learning system observes noisy or incomplete feedback, constructs a reward or target, updates its weights, policies, prompts, tools, or coordination structure under constraints, validates the resulting behavior, and changes the observations available in the next round.

Across these threads, I ask what is identifiable from imperfect feedback, how an optimization objective should be constructed when true utility is unobserved, which updates are computationally feasible, and when repeated adaptation is dynamically stable. My current research brings these questions together in models and agent systems that evaluate and modify their own behavior.
