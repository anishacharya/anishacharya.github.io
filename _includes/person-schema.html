{%- comment -%} schema.org Person, emitted on every page. Why this exists: searching "anish acharya phd" returns a Knowledge Graph card whose text describes this Anish Acharya but whose photo is Anish Acharya the a16z general partner. Two people share the name string, one has far more press coverage, and with no structured data here there was nothing telling a crawler that this site, this Scholar profile, this GitHub account and this LinkedIn account are one entity distinct from that one. `sameAs` is the property Google's entity reconciliation actually reads — a set of authoritative profile URLs it can cross-check. `disambiguatingDescription` exists in the vocabulary for exactly this case: two entities with the same name. Neither guarantees the card gets fixed (Google decides), but without them there is no signal to act on at all. Emitted site-wide rather than only on the homepage so any entry point carries the identity. `alternateName` lists every form people search the name in ("Dr. Anish Acharya", "Anish Acharya PhD", ...), so each variant reconciles to this one entity rather than to whichever Anish Acharya has the most press. `knowsAbout` entries with a Wikipedia/Wikidata `sameAs` tie the person to those topic entities, the same ones the research pages name in `about` (_data/threads.yml). ORCID and Scopus are wired in below (both in `sameAs` and as `identifier` PropertyValues). alphaXiv, Semantic Scholar (author 40026200), ResearchGate, OpenReview, DBLP (pid 120/7655) and the UT Austin Center for Big Data Analytics member page are in `sameAs`. arXiv author page (arxiv.org/a/acharya_a_3) and the Wikidata item (Q141746725) are in `sameAs` too; Wikidata is the record Google's Knowledge Graph reads most directly. Not acharya_a_1 or acharya_a_2: those are other people. More independent, cross-linking profiles is directly what makes the entity resolvable. {%- endcomment -%} {%- comment -%} schema.org WebSite.
